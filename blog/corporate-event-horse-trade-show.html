Babe Comes to San Antonio: How a White Horse Told Templeton’s Story

At the Texas Package Stores Association (TPSA) trade show in July 2025, one of our horses became the star of a Prohibition-era legend, and hundreds of guests stopped to meet her.

In Templeton, Iowa, there is a story about a white horse. During Prohibition, the story goes, the town’s whiskey makers had a quiet way of letting people know a new batch was ready: they put a white horse out front.

“If Babe the white horse was out front, it meant the whiskey was ready.”Templeton American Whiskey

Templeton tells it as a whole-town effort. German immigrants settled the town in 1882 with a taste for rye, and when Prohibition arrived, neighbors found ways to keep making it, together. Today a statue of Babe stands outside the distillery in his honor. The Templeton team also told us that bottles were once hidden inside a hollowed-out fence post, the kind of detail that makes a legend feel real.

Babe for a Day

In July 2025, Templeton Whiskey brought that story to the Texas Package Stores Association (TPSA) convention and trade show at the Henry B. Gonzalez Convention Center in San Antonio, and asked us to bring the horse. For the day, our Angel became Babe. She wore a custom-made Templeton fly sheet with their branding and spent the show greeting guests at the Templeton booth.

That’s what we do at events like this. Angel stands calmly, people pet her, and everyone takes pictures. Hundreds of attendees stopped by, and each stop was a natural opening for the Templeton team to tell their story.

Angel as “Babe” at the Templeton Whiskey booth, TPSA convention, Henry B. Gonzalez Convention Center, San Antonio, July 2025.

Why a Horse Works at a Trade Show

A trade show floor is loud, crowded, and full of booths competing for the same few seconds of attention. A banner explains a brand. A horse lets people feel it.

  • It turns a story into an experience. Templeton’s legend is about a white horse, and their guests got to meet one.
  • It gives people a reason to stop. Few people walk past a horse without slowing down, and most want a photo to share.
  • It starts conversations without a script. Nobody needs a sales pitch to ask, “Can I pet her?”
  • Custom details carry the brand. A fly sheet or blanket made with your logo and colors keeps your name in every photo.

Every venue has its own rules about animals, so we work with your event team ahead of time to plan the space, the schedule and the handling.

A Historic Stage: Stable Hall

Our horses also make regular appearances at corporate events at Stable Hall in San Antonio’s Pearl district. The brick building was designed by architect Otto Kramer and built in 1894 as a stable for roughly 70 horses that pulled beer wagons for the Pearl Brewery. In the decades since, it has been storage space, then a string of event venues, and today it is a music venue with its limestone floors and original brick walls still in place.

There is a certain poetry in bringing horses back to a building that was made for them. Angel has greeted guests at Stable Hall events, including a law firm’s corporate event where she wore a custom blanket made for the company.

Make Your Brand’s Story Walk In

Not every brand has a white horse in its history, but most have a story that is easier to feel than to explain. A horse can help tell it at:

  • Trade shows and brand activations
  • Product launches and grand openings
  • Corporate galas, holiday parties and client appreciation events
  • Promotional appearances and sponsorships

Whether your event is in San Antonio, Austin, Houston or anywhere else in Texas, tell us your story and we will help you find the right way to bring a horse into it.
